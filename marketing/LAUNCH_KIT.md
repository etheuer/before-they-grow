# Before They Grow Launch Kit

**Status:** Ready for controlled organic testing after the external gates in `RELEASE_READINESS.md`.

**Core line:** One question tonight. Their voice tomorrow.

**Audience:** Parents and guardians of children ages 3–12 who want better conversations without committing to a long journal.

## 1. Positioning

Before They Grow is a private, two-minute family ritual. It gives a parent one age-aware question, captures the answer in the child’s voice or as text, and keeps it in a dated local timeline.

### Promise

Capture the funny, thoughtful answers you will wish you could hear again.

### Proof points

- One prompt per day, selected for the child’s age band.
- One voice-recording action followed by a browser-generated, parent-editable transcript.
- No account, Before They Grow cloud storage, ad network, or social feed in this prototype.
- Local IndexedDB storage, explicit deletion, and portable JSON export with embedded audio.
- Browser speech recognition may process voice on-device or through the browser provider; every campaign must preserve that disclosure.

### What not to claim

Do not claim:

- End-to-end encryption, cloud backup, multi-device sync, or recovery.
- COPPA certification, legal approval, or independent security certification.
- App Store availability before a native package has passed review.
- Customer counts, revenue, retention, testimonials, or outcomes without direct evidence.
- That local storage is a backup; browser/site-data deletion can remove it.

## 2. Message hierarchy

1. **Emotional job:** Hear who they were, not only remember what they looked like.
2. **Behavioral promise:** One good question and one small answer tonight.
3. **Objection handling:** No long journal and no pressure to be profound.
4. **Trust proof:** Saved memories remain in this browser and support export and deletion; browser speech processing is disclosed.
5. **Action:** Try tonight’s question.

## 5. Five bounded channel experiments

Each experiment runs for 14 days or until its kill threshold is met. The prototype has no analytics, so use privacy-safe campaign-link clicks, landing-page visits from hosting logs, and voluntary tester feedback. Do not add child-level tracking.

### Channel A — Creator / parent UGC

**Hypothesis:** A parent demonstrating the question ritual will create more qualified visits than a feature tour.

**Asset:** Three 20–30 second videos from two small parenting creators.

**Script:**

> I stopped asking “How was school?” because I kept getting “fine.” Tonight’s question is: “What made you laugh the most today?” Before They Grow gives us one question, keeps the original voice, and lets me correct the browser-generated transcript. Saved memories stay in this browser; speech processing depends on the browser, and I export anything important.

**Budget cap:** $300 total only after tester consent and a functioning public URL.

**Primary metric:** Qualified landing visits per creator post.

**Secondary metric:** Voluntary first-memory confirmations.

**Kill:** Stop a creator after two posts with no qualified visits or disclosure problems.

**Scale:** Commission three more assets only if one creator generates at least 20 qualified visits and five voluntary first-memory confirmations.

### Channel B — Faceless short-form content

**Hypothesis:** Useful question prompts earn saves and shares without showing children.

**Formats:** Text-on-paper, phone lock-screen, hands-only demo, animated prompt cards.

**Cadence:** One short and one carousel daily for 14 days.

**Primary metric:** Saves per 1,000 views.

**Kill:** Retire a format after five posts below the account’s median save rate.

**Scale:** Turn the top three hooks into ten prompt variations.

### Channel C — Founder-led content

**Hypothesis:** Honest product reasoning and privacy trade-offs will attract early testers.

**Cadence:** Four posts per week on X and LinkedIn.

**Post themes:**

- Why “one question” beats an empty journal.
- Why the prototype is local-first and what that does not protect against.
- The question that replaced “How was your day?”
- Building export before billing.
- What parents say is awkward, useful, or unnecessary during testing.

**Primary metric:** Qualified replies or tester requests.

**Kill:** Change the topic mix if eight posts generate no qualified conversation.

**Scale:** Publish a weekly build note if at least five parents ask to follow progress.

### Channel D — Micro-influencer partnerships

**Hypothesis:** Parenting educators and family therapists can introduce the ritual with more trust than broad lifestyle accounts.

**Sample:** Contact 20 accounts with 5,000–75,000 followers and relevant recent content.

**Offer:** Free early access; optional fixed-fee pilot only after an audience-fit call. No commission tied to child data or recordings.

**Primary metric:** Replies from qualified partners, then qualified visits.

**Kill:** Stop after 20 well-matched contacts if fewer than two respond positively; revise the offer before further outreach.

**Scale:** Test up to five partners when at least two can explain the product accurately and accept disclosure/privacy terms.

### Channel E — Paid acquisition

**Hypothesis:** The emotional promise can earn low-cost qualified visits, but paid scale should wait for retention evidence.

**Creative cells:**

1. “Photos show how they looked. Their answer shows who they were.”
2. “Stop asking ‘How was school?’ Ask this instead.”
3. “One question tonight. Their voice tomorrow.”

**Landing cells:** Emotional hero versus practical two-minute hero.

**Budget cap:** $150 total, maximum $25/day, only after a public privacy page and campaign measurement are working.

**Primary metric:** Cost per qualified landing visit.

**Kill:** Stop any creative after $30 with zero qualified visits; stop the campaign at the cap regardless of results.

**Scale:** Do not exceed the cap until a small cohort shows voluntary week-one repeat use.

## 7. Fourteen-day operating plan

Use `CONTENT_CALENDAR.csv` for the complete 42-slot schedule.

### Days 1–3

- Publish the three core hooks.
- Invite 10–15 known parents to try one question.
- Record objections verbatim without collecting a child’s answer.

### Days 4–7

- Repeat the best hook in two formats.
- Publish the saved-memory storage and browser speech-processing explanation.
- Conduct five short parent interviews.

### Days 8–10

- Start creator and micro-influencer outreach.
- Publish one founder build note.
- Compare emotional and practical landing language.

### Days 11–14

- Run the bounded paid test only if public deployment and measurement gates are ready.
- Ask testers whether they saved three memories and why or why not.
- Decide: continue, revise the ritual, or stop.

## 8. Tester interview guide

Ask about behavior, not compliments:

1. Tell me about the last time you wished you had recorded something your child said.
2. What do you use today, if anything?
3. When did this two-minute ritual fit naturally? When did it feel forced?
4. Did the voice-first flow and editable transcript feel natural? Where did it fail?
5. What made you hesitate before saving?
6. Did you come back without a reminder?
7. What would make you export or delete everything?
8. Would you be disappointed if this disappeared tomorrow? Why?

Never ask the parent to send the child’s recording as research evidence.

## 9. Decision scorecard

At the end of 14 days, record:

- Invited parents.
- Parents who opened the product.
- Parents who saved one memory.
- Parents who saved at least three memories in seven days.
- Voluntary week-one return rate.
- Voice versus text choice.
- Export attempts and deletion attempts.
- Top three objections.
- Qualified visits and first-memory confirmations by channel.
- Spend by experiment.

### Continue criteria

Continue iterating if at least 10 parents complete onboarding, at least five save three memories in a week, no critical privacy incident occurs, and interview feedback identifies a repeatable ritual context.

### Revise criteria

Revise onboarding, prompt quality, or timing if parents value the idea but fewer than half of activated testers return twice.

### Stop criteria

Stop paid acquisition if parents will not repeat the ritual, privacy expectations cannot be met truthfully, or acquisition requires claims the product cannot substantiate.
